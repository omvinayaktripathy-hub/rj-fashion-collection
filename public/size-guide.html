📏 Size Guide

Find your perfect fit with our comprehensive measurement charts for Sarees, Kurtis, Lehengas, and Jewelry.

Jump to: 📐 How to Measure 🥻 Sarees & Blouse 👗 Kurtis ✨ Lehengas 💍 Jewelry 💡 Shopping Tips

📐 How to Take Your Measurements

Use a soft measuring tape (not a metal ruler) for the most accurate results. Measure directly on your body or over minimal clothing. Have someone assist you for back measurements. All measurements are in centimeters (cm) unless stated.

👚

Bust / Chest

  1. Stand straight, arms relaxed at sides.
  2. Wrap the tape around the fullest part of your chest.
  3. Keep the tape parallel to the floor.
  4. Breathe normally — don't hold your breath or pull in.
  5. Note the measurement in cm.
🎀

Waist

  1. Find your natural waist — the narrowest part of your torso, typically 1 inch above your navel.
  2. Wrap the tape snugly (not tight) around this point.
  3. Keep your posture natural and relaxed.
  4. Record the measurement.
🩲

Hip

  1. Stand with feet together.
  2. Measure around the fullest part of your hips and buttocks — usually 7–9 inches below your waist.
  3. Keep the tape level and parallel to the floor.
  4. Do not pull the tape too tight.
📏

Length / Height

  1. For kurti/dress length: measure from your shoulder (at the neck point) straight down to the desired hemline.
  2. For inseam/length: stand straight, measure from the crotch to the ankle.
  3. Wear the footwear you intend to pair with the garment while measuring.
💪

Shoulder Width

  1. Stand naturally with arms at sides.
  2. Measure across the back from the edge of one shoulder to the edge of the other.
  3. This is the top seam measurement, not armhole.
  4. Useful for blouse and kurti tops.
💍

Wrist / Ring Size

  1. For bangles: wrap a thread around your knuckles (all four fingers together) — this determines the bangle size.
  2. For rings: wrap a thin paper strip around your finger, mark where it meets, and measure the length in mm.
  3. Measure in the evening when fingers are at their largest.

⚠️ Measurement Tips

  • Always measure in centimeters (cm) for precision. The conversion is: 1 inch = 2.54 cm.
  • Do not suck in your stomach — measure naturally.
  • If you are between two sizes, always size up for comfort in traditional Indian wear.
  • For custom stitching orders, take measurements with the undergarments you intend to wear.
  • Re-check measurements every 6 months if your weight changes significantly.

How to Drape a Saree (Nivi Style — Most Common)

1
Tuck the Start

Start with the plain end of the saree. Tuck it into the petticoat at your right hip, going once around your waist.

2
Make Pleats

Make 5–7 even pleats (each about 5 inches wide). Tuck them at your navel into the petticoat, angled slightly to the left.

3
Drape Across

Take the remaining fabric across your waist from right to left, wrapping it once around your body.

4
Pallu Over Shoulder

Drape the pallu (decorated end) over your left shoulder from front to back. Adjust length so it falls elegantly.

5
Pin & Secure

Use safety pins at the shoulder seam and at the pleats to secure the drape. Adjust for a clean, even look.

6
Final Adjustments

Ensure the hemline is even — about 1 inch above the ground. Fluff pleats and pallu for a graceful finish.

💡 Tips for Online Shopping — Indian Ethnic Wear

🛍️ Before You Order

  • Always measure yourself fresh — don't rely on measurements from months ago.
  • Read the product description carefully for fabric type (pure silk, art silk, georgette, etc.) as they fit differently.
  • Check the weight of the fabric (grams per meter) — heavier silk sarees (600–900 gsm) drape differently than lighter ones (300–500 gsm).
  • For festive and bridal wear, order 2–3 weeks in advance to allow time for any alterations.
  • Check the number of product photos — more photos mean higher confidence in color and fabric representation.

🪡 Fabric & Fit Insights

  • Pure Silk Sarees (Kanjivaram, Banarasi) — Stiff when new, soften beautifully with wear. They hold pleats better than lighter fabrics.
  • Georgette & Chiffon Sarees — Flow beautifully but may need extra pins for pleating. Great for parties and casual events.
  • Kurtis with embroidery — May feel slightly stiffer at the embroidered areas. Choose 1 size up for full comfort.
  • Lehenga Skirts — Always have a 2"–3" alteration margin at the hem. If unsure about length, go slightly longer — it's easier to shorten than lengthen.
  • Silk Blouses — Silk has minimal stretch. For a comfortable fit, add 1"–2" to your actual bust measurement when selecting a size.
  • Jewelry Fit — Bangles should slip over your hand knuckles with slight resistance — too easy = will fall off; too tight = uncomfortable.

🌿 Care & Maintenance Tips

  • Dry-clean pure silk sarees; hand-wash art silk, georgette, and cotton sarees gently.
  • Always store sarees and lehengas folded in muslin cotton cloth — avoid plastic covers that trap moisture.
  • Store silver jewelry in anti-tarnish pouches. Wipe with a soft dry cloth after each wear.
  • Air your garments before storing — especially after festive events where you may have perspired.
  • For embellished garments, fold with the embroidery facing inward to protect the zari and stone work.
  • Never iron directly on zari embroidery — use a pressing cloth or steam from a safe distance.
